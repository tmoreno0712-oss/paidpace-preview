3 polite overdue invoice emails you can actually send.

It’s awkward to ask a client for money they already owe. These examples help you follow up clearly and professionally without sounding aggressive. Replace the bracketed fields before sending.

1. Friendly reminder: 1–7 days overdue

Use a light touch when an invoice has just passed its due date. The client may simply have overlooked it.

Subject: Friendly reminder — invoice [INVOICE NUMBER]

Hi [CLIENT NAME], I hope you're doing well! I'm checking in about invoice [INVOICE NUMBER] for [AMOUNT], due on [DUE DATE]. It looks like the payment hasn't come through yet. Could you let me know whether it's in process? I'm happy to resend the invoice if that would help. Thanks so much, [YOUR NAME]

2. Professional follow-up: 8–30 days overdue

Be specific about the invoice and ask for a payment update or expected date. Leave room for honest mistakes or processing delays.

Subject: Payment status request — invoice [INVOICE NUMBER]

Hi [CLIENT NAME], I'm following up on invoice [INVOICE NUMBER] for [AMOUNT], originally due on [DUE DATE]. My records still show it as unpaid. Could you please confirm its payment status and when I should expect payment? If there's an issue with the invoice details, let me know so we can resolve it. Thank you, [YOUR NAME]

3. Clear but respectful reminder: 31+ days overdue

For older unpaid invoices, communicate that you need a concrete response. Avoid threatening fees or collection actions you aren't legally entitled or prepared to take.

Subject: Follow-up needed — outstanding invoice [INVOICE NUMBER]

Hi [CLIENT NAME], Invoice [INVOICE NUMBER] for [AMOUNT], due on [DUE DATE], remains outstanding in my records. Please reply with an expected payment date or let me know if there's a concern we need to discuss. I'd appreciate an update by [RESPONSE DATE] so we can agree on next steps. Thank you for your attention to this. [YOUR NAME]

What to include in a payment reminder

  • The invoice number, amount, and original due date
  • A clear request: an update, expected payment date, or confirmation
  • A polite tone that matches your client relationship
  • A way for the client to flag an incorrect invoice or payment already sent
  • Only payment terms and fees that actually apply to your agreement

How often should you follow up?

There's no universal schedule. A common workflow is a friendly reminder shortly after the due date, another follow-up around a week later, and a more direct request if the balance remains unpaid. Match your approach to your contract, payment terms, and customer relationship.
